Welcome to my bakery

A Singaporean twist on a Japanese favourite.

These shio pans are crispy and golden on the outside, soft and fluffy on the inside, with a glorious buttery centre and a salty finish. Made with butter, patience, and just a little bit of chaos. 🧈🥐

The recipe

What you'll need 🧺

⏱Active timeAbout 1 hour
🌙ProofingOvernight + final proofs
🔥Bake200°C • 20 min
🥐Yield12 shio pans
530 gBread flour
410 gCold milk
8 gYeast
10 gPink Himalayan rock salt
31 gWhite sugar
12 × 15 gUnsalted butter
As neededSalt flakes
💧 For handling: Keep some water nearby to lightly wet your hands when the dough gets sticky. The water is not part of the dough!
Before you start

Your baking station 🧑🏻‍🍳

🧰 Equipment

Stand mixer, mixing bowl, clean work surface, rolling pin, kitchen scale, baking tray, cling wrap, oven, and a water spray bottle if available.

🧊 A little prep

Have your butter ready to portion and chill. Keep the milk cold, and make sure there is enough space in your container for the dough to expand.

📏 Portion guide

Aim for 12 portions of about 80 g each. Small differences are okay because a little dough can be lost during handling.

Step by step

Let's make some shiok-pan 👩🏻‍🍳

01

Dump it all in!

Add bread flour, salt, sugar and yeast to the stand mixer bowl. Add the cold milk last.

02

Let the mixer do the work.

Set the stand mixer to Speed 2 and knead for 10 minutes.

03

Slap, stretch & fold.

Clean your work surface and place the dough down. Stretch and fold it, giving it a few good slaps against the table to help it come together.

04

First proof + stretch & fold.

Proof for 15 minutes. Then stretch and fold from all four sides towards the centre. Let it rest again.

05

Round two!

Let the dough proof again, then perform the second stretch and fold.

06

Overnight beauty sleep 🌙

Cover with cling wrap, leaving room for the dough to grow. Proof overnight until doubled, then place it in the fridge.

07

Divide & conquer.

Take the dough out of the fridge and divide into 12 portions of about 80 g each.

08

Give them a little rest.

Shape each portion into a circular ball and proof for 15 minutes, or until approximately doubled in size.

09

Get your butter ready 🧈

Cut the butter into 12 pieces of 15 g each and return them to the freezer so they stay cold.

10

Shape the dough.

Roll each dough ball so one end is thinner and the other is thicker, creating an elongated inverted-triangle shape.

11

Butter goes in!

Place one 15 g piece of cold butter at the wide end. Roll tightly from the wide end towards the thin end to form your shio pan.

12

One last proof.

Preheat the oven to 200°C with top and bottom heating for 25 minutes. While it preheats, let the shaped shio pans proof until doubled.

13

The finishing touch ✨

Lightly mist the shio pans with water and sprinkle salt flakes over the tops.

14

BAKE! 🔥

Bake at 200°C for 20 minutes, until the butter has caramelised around the bread and the tops are beautifully golden brown.

Baker's tips

Little tricks for better bread 💡

💦 No water spray bottle?
Place an oven-safe tray at the bottom of the oven while preheating. When you start baking, carefully add ice cubes to the hot tray and immediately close the oven door. The steam helps create a lovely crust.
🧈 Keep your butter COLD.
Return the butter pieces to the freezer while you work with the dough. Cold butter helps keep the buttery centre in place while shaping.
💧 Sticky dough?
Lightly wet your hands with water before handling the dough. Just a little moisture is enough!
Recipe notes

Good to know 📖

🥐 What is shio pan?

"Shio" means salt in Japanese. Shio pan is a soft bread known for its buttery centre and salty, crisp finish.

🧈 The butter trick

The butter tucked inside melts during baking, helping create the signature rich centre and buttery base.

⏳ Don't rush the dough

Proofing times can vary with temperature and dough conditions. Use the dough's size and appearance as your guide.

Frequently asked

FAQ 💬

Can I make these without a stand mixer?

Yes, you can knead by hand, but expect it to take longer. Focus on developing a smooth, stretchy dough.

Why is my dough so dry?

If your dough feels unusually dry or difficult to bring together, check that all the milk was added and that the flour was measured accurately. Flour can absorb liquid differently depending on the environment. Avoid adding lots of extra flour while kneading — lightly wet your hands if the dough is sticking.

Why is my dough not proofing?

Proofing can be slower when the dough or room is cool. Give the dough more time and look for an increase in size rather than relying only on the clock. Also check that your yeast is still active and that the dough has been covered so it does not dry out.

Why is the milk cold?

Cold milk helps keep the dough temperature from rising too quickly during mixing.

Why does the butter need to stay cold?

Cold butter is easier to wrap inside the dough and helps keep the filling where you want it during shaping.

How do I know the dough has doubled?

Look for a clear increase in volume rather than relying only on the clock. Room temperature can change proofing speed.
